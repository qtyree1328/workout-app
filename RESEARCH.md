# Exercise organization — research and editorial decisions

Reviewed September 24, 2026. The references below inform the structure, not individual ratings or the exact routines.

## Principles used

1. **Separate movement difficulty, body area, and equipment.** ACE uses distinct body-part, experience-level, and equipment categories. This library follows that organization. Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced describe estimated coordination, balance, strength prerequisites, and range demands of the displayed movement. They are not a guarantee of suitability. [ACE exercise library](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/)
2. **Effort depends on the person and dose.** CDC distinguishes relative effort from the activity itself. Light / Moderate / High are editorial estimates for a short, controlled bout using the displayed setup, not measured physiological intensity, injury risk, or a conversion of the CDC aerobic talk test to strength training. Pace, band resistance, hold duration, rest, and ability change effort. Users can override the labels. [CDC intensity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html)
3. **Keep groups simple and adaptable.** ACSM's 2026 guidance emphasizes regular participation and individualization, and supports bodyweight and resistance-band training. These groups organize this collection by purpose and equipment; they do not claim an optimal sequence for every person. [ACSM 2026 update](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)

## Application to this collection

- Arms is the requested compact upper-body bucket; the detail view retains shoulders/back/chest context. Legs includes hips. Abs includes trunk control. Full body identifies integrated movement. These are primary navigation categories, not exhaustive muscle activation claims.
- Groups are manually ordered: simple movement/preparation before more demanding control; consistent equipment and floor positions where practical. Demanding strength/skill practice remains its own group with longer editable breaks.
- Group difficulty and strain display the highest movement rating. They do not claim to predict whole-session exertion. Extra rounds or short rests can make the session substantially harder.
- The catalog now contains 54 routines spanning mobility and general strength; the expanded rules and evidence are documented below.
- The default work, repetition, and rest values are adjustable interface presets, not dosages extracted from research or prescriptions. There is no automatic progression or claim to treat pain/posture.
- Timed and rep-based work are separate. Rep mode is manually completed; the app cannot see or count physical repetitions. Static holds remain timed. For unilateral movements, settings apply to the displayed step: duplicate and label left/right to give each side its own interval, or alternate within the interval.
- Individual metadata is in `data/classification.json`, generated by `scripts/classify.py`. The labels are estimates inferred from the footage and can be changed locally. Earlier descriptive exercise names and approximate clip boundaries still need review.

## Expanded rules and examples

The catalog now has 105 entries: the 91 original movements, 13 new NHS photo guides, and an instruction-only easy-walking interval. Photos are never presented as motion recordings. Source links and credits are retained. The new examples cover chair sit-to-stand, supported mini squat, calf raise, side leg lift, standing hip extension, wall push-up, biceps curl, neck rotation/stretch, calf stretch, seated chest stretch, ankle pumps, and seated thoracic rotation. The supported versions are intentionally accessible options; they do not imply every user needs rehabilitation.

### Evidence → implementation

| Principle | Source | Applied rule |
|---|---|---|
| Gradual preparation and recovery | [American Heart Association warm-up/cool-down](https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/exercise-and-physical-activity/fitness-basics/warm-up-cool-down) | Strength plans start and finish with five minutes of easy walking, with targeted preparation between walking and main work. |
| Warm muscles before stretching; brief comfortable holds | [AHA flexibility guidance](https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/exercise-and-physical-activity/fitness-basics/flexibility-exercise-stretching) | Mobility plans begin with easy movement. Static stretch steps use 30 seconds per side. Dynamic drills have separate practice windows. Five-minute options are short movement breaks, not complete training sessions. |
| Recovery depends on training goal and experience | [Grgic et al., systematic review, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28933024/) | General strength sets retain 60–90-second breaks. More demanding standalone skills use longer default recovery and are excluded from general templates. Extend rest if needed; these are not maximal-strength routines. |
| Consistency and individualization; regular work across major muscles | [ACSM 2026 guidance](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/) | Simple plans with 1–3 sets, editable settings, and separate mobility/strength intent. Work toward regular major-muscle training at least twice weekly; selecting one small-area group is not equivalent to training the whole body. |
| Clear setup and manageable supported movements | [NHS strength](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/strength-exercises/), [NHS flexibility](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/flexibility-exercises/), [NHS sitting exercises](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/sitting-exercises/) | Added actual source photos with short paraphrased technique cues. Brief neck/chest holds are repeated gently within the practice window, not held continuously for the whole timer. |

These sources do **not** establish a universally optimal “hips for X seconds, then back” sequence. The app therefore distinguishes published principles from its own design choices. Exact exercise combinations, practice-window lengths, and category assignments remain editorial estimates, not trial-validated routines or treatment recommendations.

### Explicit catalog fields

Every entry has anatomical regions, search tags and aliases, a movement pattern, training type, laterality, eligible session phases, equipment requirements, estimated difficulty/strain, and a dosing preset with rationale. Active mobility, static stretching, strength, isometrics, motor control, and advanced skills are distinct. A 30-second stretch is not treated as equivalent to a 30-second planche.

Search matches words across these fields, normalizes common singular/plural and anatomical synonyms, requires all meaningful words for multiword queries, and tolerates a one-character typo for longer words. It is a local keyword index, not an unlimited natural-language system. For instance, “hips,” “upper back,” “shoulder band,” “calves,” “abs,” “bar shoulders,” and “no equipment core” work. No diagnosis/pain-relief tags or disease-specific treatment claims were added.

### How the 54 plans are built

- Six mobility families each have 5-, 10-, 15-, 20-, and 30-minute budget options: hips; back/shoulders; full body; desk movement; ankles/lower legs; shoulders with the long bar. Longer plans add bounded practice sets and relevant movements, not arbitrarily prolonged stretches.
- Six strength families each have 15-, 20-, 30-, and 45-minute budget options: supported full body; band/bodyweight; upper body; legs/balance; core/hips; pulling/grip. Pulling/grip uses the pull-up bar and bands, not small-edge climbing holds or ab-roller progressions that were not assessed or demonstrated here.
- Warm-up happens once, then the main block uses up to three sets per movement, followed by the ending block. Left/right work is added as balanced pairs. Set order cycles through compatible patterns before repeating. This is a practical sequencing choice, not a mandatory physiological rule.
- Strength work uses a controlled-repetition target inside a bounded time window. Finish the target and rest for the remaining window; do not rush or work to failure just because time remains. The fixed window makes a time-budget routine predictable. Rep-count mode is available but then duration is unknown.
- Individual work, holds, recovery, and side balance are checked by tests. Do not shorten recovery or stretch longer just to fill the slider's budget.
- The long bar is treated as an unweighted dowel. Default equipment matching includes bands, that bar, pull-up bar, chair, wall, and yoga block. Pads do not change the required equipment list. Dumbbell examples remain searchable but are not used in the built-in routines.

### Time accounting

Displayed duration includes the three-second countdown, every timed work interval, every break that actually occurs, and warm-up/cool-down. There is no final break. Plans can be shorter than their nominal budget: a “30-minute option” may take 28–30 minutes. Eleven options take 27–30 minutes; six take 40–45 minutes. Longer plans are not necessarily harder, because recovery and preparation count too.

The time slider filters globally and sorts by planned duration descending, including user-created timed groups. Untimed rep-based groups are excluded under a finite budget and are available through “Any length.” Pausing, skipping, additional rest, or switching to manual reps changes actual elapsed time. Overriding main work does not replace the warm-up/cool-down timing.

## Climbing training

Added September 25, 2026. Equipment: a hangboard and a pull-up bar. Open the **Climbing** tab, or use the **Climbing** chip on any page. Every climbing exercise and protocol is labelled with what it trains. The app times each protocol; you choose the load (added weight, a smaller edge, or feet on a chair to take weight off). It cannot measure force.

### What the evidence supports

| Protocol in the app | Trains | Evidence | How the app runs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Max hangs · half crimp** | Max finger strength, crimps | López-Rivera & González-Badillo, 2012 (Sports Technology) and [2019 (J Hum Kinet)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/324731332_Comparison_of_the_Effects_of_Three_Hangboard_Strength_and_Endurance_Training_Programs_on_Grip_Endurance_in_Sport_Climbers): 8 weeks of 10-s added-weight hangs improved grip endurance by ~34%. [Mundry et al. 2021 (Sci Rep RCT)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-92898-2): hangs with weight added progressively (+1.25 kg/week) improved grip strength vs climbing only. | Warm-up hangs, then 5 × 10 s with 3-min rests. Load so ~3 s stays in reserve. |
| **Max hangs · grip rotation** | Max finger strength, crimps, slopers & pockets | [Levernier & Laffaye 2019 (JSCR)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28945641/): 4 weeks of small-hold hangs raised max force and rate of force development in elite boulderers, measured separately by grip type (slope, half crimp, full crimp). | 3 rounds alternating half crimp and open hand, 10 s, 3-min rests. |
| **Min-edge hangs** | Crimps (small holds) | López-Rivera MaxHangs phase 2: bodyweight on the smallest edge that can be held ~10 s with a reserve. | 5 × 10 s, 3-min rests. |
| **Repeaters 7:3** (4 or 3 sets) | Strength-endurance / power endurance (pumpy routes) | [Medernach et al. 2015 (JSCR)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26203738/): 4 weeks of intermittent fingerboard training added ~26 s to finger-hang endurance. 7:3 structure: [Lattice Training](https://www.climbing.com/skills/lattice-hangboarding-part-2/) (coaching protocol, not itself a trial). | 6 × (7 s on / 3 s off) per set, 3-min rests. |
| **Intermittent hangs · hard (80%)** | Strength-endurance + max strength | [Devise et al. 2022 (Front Sports Act Living RCT)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.862782/full): 10 s on / 6 s off at 80% of max, up to 12 reps, 3 sets, 8-min rests, 2×/week for 4 weeks. Improved max strength and endurance. | Same protocol exactly. |
| **Endurance hangs · moderate (60%)** | Endurance / stamina | Devise et al. 2022: 10 s on / 6 s off at 60%, 24 reps, 2 sets, 6-min rest. Mainly improved endurance. | Same protocol exactly. |
| **Low-intensity hangs** | Finger strength, tendon & pulley capacity | [Gilmore et al. 2024 (Sports Med Open)](https://sportsmedicine-open.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40798-024-00793-7): frequent 10-min low-intensity hanging (“Abrahangs”) improved strength about as much as max hangs; combined was additive. **Observational** training-log data, not an RCT. | 20 × (10 s on / 20 s off), feet assisted. |
| **Pulling strength** | Pulling strength | Pull-up capacity discriminates climbing grade ([2023 testing review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2023.1130812/full)). [2024 RCT in advanced climbers (Bioengineering)](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5354/11/1/85): eccentric pull-up training raised strength, speed and range. | 4 heavy sets of 3–5 weighted pull-ups (3-min rest) + 3 sets of slow negatives. |
| **Pulling power** | Power, contact strength (dynamic moves) | Same 2024 RCT: plyometric (explosive) pull-ups raised velocity, power and work. | 6 sets × 3 fast reps, 2.5-min rest. |
| **Lock-off strength** | Lock-offs | Same 2024 RCT: isometric pull-up training increased max strength. | 5 sets of 5-s holds at the top, 90° and 120°, + negatives. |
| **Core tension for steep climbing** | Core tension | General strength practice: no climbing-specific core trial was found. Editorial pairing. | Hanging knee raises + hollow holds, 3 rounds. |
| **Antagonists & shoulder health** | Antagonists, shoulder health | Common coaching practice; no climbing-specific trial. | Push-ups + band W raises + band lateral raises, 3 rounds. |
| **Climbing strength session** | Max finger strength + pulling + antagonists | [Stien et al. 2023 meta-analysis (Biol Sport)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36636194/): specific resistance training can improve climbing performance; the evidence base is still small. | Fingers first: 4 max hangs, then 3 weighted pull-up sets, then 2 push-up sets. |

### Programming rules used

- **Warm up first.** Every finger protocol starts with easy movement, scapular pull-ups and 3–4 progressive warm-up hangs from ~40% to ~80% effort. Tests check that no max hang comes before the warm-up hangs.
- **Frequency.** The trials used 2 sessions a week for 4–8 weeks. Keep at least 48 hours between hard finger sessions. Low-intensity hangs are the exception and can be done more often.
- **Rest.** Max-intensity hangs keep ≥3 minutes between efforts (tested). Intermittent protocols keep the published set rests.
- **One focus per block.** For example, max hangs for 4 weeks, then repeaters or intermittent hangs. Retest before switching, as in the López-Rivera programs.
- **Grip position.** The half crimp is the default training grip; open hand is trained separately because strength is grip-specific. Full crimp is not programmed.
- **Limits.** Most study participants were intermediate to elite climbers. Hangboarding is generally advised only after about a year of regular climbing, and not for young climbers who are still growing. Stop for sharp or localized finger pain. Core and antagonist sessions are editorial, labelled as general strength practice.
- Diagrams are simple how-to illustrations, not recordings. Each exercise's details link to its study.

## 21-Day Hip Opening Challenge

The supplied YOGABODY pose chart (`Posechart_Hip-Challenge.pdf`, 42 poses, two per day) is added as photo guides. Each photo shows the main pose plus the easier variations and props from the chart. It appears as **Hip Challenge · Day 1–21** under Groups (type filter: *21-day hip challenge*), and every pose is searchable in Exercises (for example “pigeon”, “butterfly”, “day 5”).

- Each day: 2 minutes of easy walking, 90/90 hip switches and a half-kneeling hip shift as a warm-up, then each pose held **2 × 30 s** (each side for one-sided poses). That accumulates the ~60 s per stretch that the [ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21694556/) suggests (10–30 s holds, 2–3+ days per week). The chart itself gives no hold times; these timings are the app's.
- Props shown in the chart (block, chair, strap, table, pole) are optional, so the days are not hidden by the equipment filter.
- Regenerate the images with `python3 scripts/add_hip_poses.py --password …` (requires `pip install pymupdf`). The password is not stored in the repository.
