# Exercise organization — research and editorial decisions

Reviewed September 25, 2026. The references below inform the structure, not individual ratings or the exact routines.

## Principles used

1. **Separate movement difficulty, body area, and equipment.** ACE uses distinct body-part, experience-level, and equipment categories. This library follows that organization. Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced describe estimated coordination, balance, strength prerequisites, and range demands of the displayed movement. They are not a guarantee of suitability. [ACE exercise library](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/)
2. **Effort depends on the person and dose.** CDC distinguishes relative effort from the activity itself. Light / Moderate / High are editorial estimates for a short, controlled bout using the displayed setup, not measured physiological intensity, injury risk, or a conversion of the CDC aerobic talk test to strength training. Pace, band resistance, hold duration, rest, and ability change effort. Users can override the labels. [CDC intensity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html)
3. **Keep groups simple and adaptable.** ACSM's 2026 guidance emphasizes regular participation and individualization, and supports bodyweight and resistance-band training. These groups organize this collection by purpose and equipment; they do not claim an optimal sequence for every person. [ACSM 2026 update](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)

## Session catalogue

Curated workouts, organized by category. Each has a warm-up, main block, and cool-down (or cool-down omitted for Hip Opener). Pick your time; the app adapts sets, flow repeats, or warm-up and cool-down to fit.

### Climbing

- **Max Hangs** — Five heavy 10-second hangs in a half crimp.
- **Grip Rotation** — Alternate half crimp and open hand at max-hang intensity.
- **Min-Edge Hangs** — Bodyweight on the smallest edge you can hold for ~13 s.
- **Repeaters 7:3** — 4 sets of six 7-second hangs with 3-second breaks.
- **Repeaters · Short** — Three repeater sets when time is tight.
- **Intermittent Hangs · 80%** — Devise protocol: 10 s on, 6 s off, 12 hangs, 3 sets.
- **Endurance Hangs · 60%** — Two long sets of 24 moderate hangs for stamina.
- **Daily Easy Hangs** — Ten minutes of gentle, feet-assisted hangs.
- **Pulling Strength** — Heavy pull-ups, then slow negatives.
- **Pulling Power** — Explosive pull-ups for dynamic moves.
- **Lock-Off Strength** — Holds at the top, 90° and 120°, then negatives.
- **Core Tension** — Keep your feet on steep terrain.
- **Antagonists & Shoulders** — Push and rotator-cuff work to balance the pulling.
- **Full Climbing Strength** — Fingers, then heavy pulling, then pushing.
- **Pre-Climb Warm-Up** — Ten minutes before you tie in or boulder.

### Strength

- **Upper Body Push & Pull** — Pull-ups and push-ups in supersets, then band work.
- **Push Strength** — Calisthenics pushing: pike, archer and diamond push-ups.
- **Calisthenics Skills** — Frog stand, L-sit, tuck front lever and handstand.
- **Legs & Balance** — Single-leg strength and hip stability.
- **Core Circuit** — Seven exercises, 30–40 s each, two rounds.
- **Bodyweight Full Body** — No equipment. Three rounds, twenty minutes.

### Mobility

- **Hip Mobility Flow** — Rotation, flexion and extension through the hips.
- **Climber's Hips** — Turn-out and deep flexion for high steps and drop knees.
- **Shoulders & Upper Back** — Shoulder-blade control and thoracic rotation with a dowel.
- **Hamstrings & Splits** — Forward folds and split-line holds.
- **Morning Mobility** — Nine standing minutes to wake everything up.
- **Full-Body Mobility** — Spine, shoulders and hips in twenty minutes.

### Recovery

- **Post-Climb Cool-Down** — Shake out the arms and let the hips go.
- **Evening Unwind** — Floor-only passive holds before bed.
- **Desk Reset** — Five minutes for neck, chest and back.
- **Five-Minute Hip Reset** — A quick fix for stiff hips.

### Hip Opener

**Hip Opener** — Random poses from the 42-pose hip chart: 6-minute holds, 1-minute rests. Pick a time budget and number of poses. Poses are ordered from standing to lying, so you go down to the floor once. Each one-sided pose is held 3 minutes per side (10-second switch). Two-sided poses are held 6 minutes. One minute of rest separates poses. This is a yin-style hold: 6 minutes far exceeds the ~60-second stretch minimum from ACSM. Ease in, use props, and pick the easier variation on the photo guide. Back off any pinching or joint pain. Shuffle or swap single poses before starting.

## Climbing training

Added September 25, 2026. Equipment: a hangboard and a pull-up bar. Open the **Climbing** tab, or use the **Climbing** chip on any page. Every climbing exercise and protocol is labelled with what it trains. The app times each protocol; you choose the load (added weight, a smaller edge, or feet on a chair to take weight off). It cannot measure force.

### What the evidence supports

| Protocol in the app | Trains | Evidence | How the app runs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Max hangs · half crimp** | Max finger strength, crimps | López-Rivera & González-Badillo, 2012 (Sports Technology) and [2019 (J Hum Kinet)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/324731332_Comparison_of_the_Effects_of_Three_Hangboard_Strength_and_Endurance_Training_Programs_on_Grip_Endurance_in_Sport_Climbers): 8 weeks of 10-s added-weight hangs improved grip endurance by ~34%. [Mundry et al. 2021 (Sci Rep RCT)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-92898-2): hangs with weight added progressively (+1.25 kg/week) improved grip strength vs climbing only. | Warm-up hangs, then 5 × 10 s with 3-min rests. Load so ~3 s stays in reserve. |
| **Max hangs · grip rotation** | Max finger strength, crimps, slopers & pockets | [Levernier & Laffaye 2019 (JSCR)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28945641/): 4 weeks of small-hold hangs raised max force and rate of force development in elite boulderers, measured separately by grip type (slope, half crimp, full crimp). | 3 rounds alternating half crimp and open hand, 10 s, 3-min rests. |
| **Min-edge hangs** | Crimps (small holds) | López-Rivera MaxHangs phase 2: bodyweight on the smallest edge that can be held ~10 s with a reserve. | 5 × 10 s, 3-min rests. |
| **Repeaters 7:3** (4 or 3 sets) | Strength-endurance / power endurance (pumpy routes) | [Medernach et al. 2015 (JSCR)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26203738/): 4 weeks of intermittent fingerboard training added ~26 s to finger-hang endurance. 7:3 structure: [Lattice Training](https://www.climbing.com/skills/lattice-hangboarding-part-2/) (coaching protocol, not itself a trial). | 6 × (7 s on / 3 s off) per set, 3-min rests. |
| **Intermittent hangs · hard (80%)** | Strength-endurance + max strength | [Devise et al. 2022 (Front Sports Act Living RCT)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.862782/full): 10 s on / 6 s off at 80% of max, up to 12 reps, 3 sets, 8-min rests, 2×/week for 4 weeks. Improved max strength and endurance. | Same protocol exactly. |
| **Endurance hangs · moderate (60%)** | Endurance / stamina | Devise et al. 2022: 10 s on / 6 s off at 60%, 24 reps, 2 sets, 6-min rest. Mainly improved endurance. | Same protocol exactly. |
| **Low-intensity hangs** | Finger strength, tendon & pulley capacity | [Gilmore et al. 2024 (Sports Med Open)](https://sportsmedicine-open.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40798-024-00793-7): frequent 10-min low-intensity hanging ("Abrahangs") improved strength about as much as max hangs; combined was additive. **Observational** training-log data, not an RCT. | 20 × (10 s on / 20 s off), feet assisted. |
| **Pulling strength** | Pulling strength | Pull-up capacity discriminates climbing grade ([2023 testing review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2023.1130812/full)). [2024 RCT in advanced climbers (Bioengineering)](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5354/11/1/85): eccentric pull-up training raised strength, speed and range. | 4 heavy sets of 3–5 weighted pull-ups (3-min rest) + 3 sets of slow negatives. |
| **Pulling power** | Power, contact strength (dynamic moves) | Same 2024 RCT: plyometric (explosive) pull-ups raised velocity, power and work. | 6 sets × 3 fast reps, 2.5-min rest. |
| **Lock-off strength** | Lock-offs | Same 2024 RCT: isometric pull-up training increased max strength. | 5 sets of 5-s holds at the top, 90° and 120°, + negatives. |
| **Core tension for steep climbing** | Core tension | General strength practice: no climbing-specific core trial was found. Editorial pairing. | Hanging knee raises + hollow holds, 3 rounds. |
| **Antagonists & shoulder health** | Antagonists, shoulder health | Common coaching practice; no climbing-specific trial. | Push-ups + band W raises + band lateral raises, 3 rounds. |
| **Climbing strength session** | Max finger strength + pulling + antagonists | [Stien et al. 2023 meta-analysis (Biol Sport)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36636194/): specific resistance training can improve climbing performance; the evidence base is still small. | Fingers first: 4 max hangs, then 3 weighted pull-up sets, then 2 push-up sets. |

### Programming rules used

- **Warm up first.** Every finger protocol starts with easy movement, scapular pull-ups and 3–4 progressive warm-up hangs from ~40% to ~80% effort. Tests check that no max hang comes before the warm-up hangs.
- **Frequency.** The trials used 2 sessions a week for 4–8 weeks. Keep at least 48 hours between hard finger sessions. Low-intensity hangs are the exception and can be done more often.
- **Rest.** Max-intensity hangs keep ≥3 minutes between efforts (tested). Intermittent protocols keep the published set rests. Grip Rotation uses 3-minute rests like the other max-hang protocols.
- **One focus per block.** For example, max hangs for 4 weeks, then repeaters or intermittent hangs. Retest before switching, as in the López-Rivera programs.
- **Grip position.** The half crimp is the default training grip; open hand is trained separately because strength is grip-specific. Full crimp is not programmed.
- **Limits.** Most study participants were intermediate to elite climbers. Hangboarding is generally advised only after about a year of regular climbing, and not for young climbers who are still growing. Stop for sharp or localized finger pain. Core and antagonist sessions are editorial, labelled as general strength practice.
- Diagrams are simple how-to illustrations, not recordings. Each exercise's details link to its study.

## Foot training

Added September 26, 2026. Equipment: none, or a towel/small ball/resistance band/wall as noted per exercise. Open the **Feet** tile — it works like Hip Opener: pick a time and a number of exercises, and the app draws at random from a 20-exercise foot & ankle pool, ordered mobility → intrinsic → strength → balance → stretch so a session flows sensibly. No illustrations yet (drawings arrive later); each exercise is an instruction card with a how-to note.

### What the evidence supports

| Group in the pool | Exercises | Evidence | How the app runs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mobility** | Ankle circles, knee-to-wall ankle mobilization | Weight-bearing lunge / [knee-to-wall test](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Knee_to_Wall_Test): a reliable measure of ankle dorsiflexion, and the same motion doubles as a mobilization drill; ~3.6° of dorsiflexion per centimetre the knee travels past the toes. | Ankle circles 30 s each direction/foot; knee-to-wall 10 reps each foot. |
| **Intrinsic foot muscles** | Short foot hold, toe splay & lift, toe spread & squeeze, towel curls, marble pickups | [McKeon, Hertel, Bramble & Davis 2015 (Br J Sports Med)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24659509/): the "foot core system" — intrinsic foot muscles are local stabilizers of the arch, trained by isolating them (the short-foot exercise) and by toe-flexion work (towel curls, object pickups). | Short-foot holds 10 s; toe drills and towel/marble work as timed rep sets. |
| **Strength** | Double-leg, single-leg and bent-knee (soleus) heel raises, heel raises with toes elevated, tibialis raises, ankle eversion/inversion with a band, toe & heel walks | [Baxter, Corrigan, Hullfish et al. 2017 — Achilles tendon loading review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5343533/): heel-raise/-lower variations progressively load the Achilles and calf, from an easy double-leg raise up through single-leg and toe-elevated versions; bending the knee shifts load onto the soleus. Tibialis raises and band eversion/inversion balance the plantarflexor-heavy heel-raise work with the muscles that lift the foot and resist rolling an ankle. | Heel-raise variants as timed sets of ~12–15 reps; tibialis raises and band work similarly; toe/heel walks as a 40 s timed drill. |
| **Balance** | Single-leg balance, single-leg balance with eyes closed | [Balance training in chronic ankle instability — systematic review & meta-analysis, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10860262/): single-leg and proprioceptive balance training improves postural control and self-reported ankle function and lowers re-sprain rates; removing vision (eyes closed) increases the demand on the ankle's own balance receptors. | 30 s / 20 s single-leg holds, each side. |
| **Stretch** | Foot rolling on a ball, calf stretch (straight knee / bent knee), plantar fascia stretch | [DiGiovanni et al. 2006 (J Bone Joint Surg, 2-year follow-up RCT)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16882901/): a plantar-fascia-specific stretch (pulling the toes back to tension the arch) outperformed a calf-stretch-only protocol for chronic heel pain, with benefits holding at two years — hence a dedicated toe-extension stretch alongside the calf stretches. | 30 s holds, each side; ball rolling 45 s each foot. |

### Programming rules used

- **Group order, not a fixed routine.** The pool's `group` field (mobility, intrinsic, strength, balance, stretch) sorts whatever the generator draws, so a 4-exercise session and a 10-exercise session both build sensibly instead of jumping randomly between a stretch and a balance drill.
- **Own dose per exercise.** Unlike Hip Opener's fixed 6-minute hold, each foot/ankle exercise keeps its own work time, rest and (for rep-based moves) target reps from `data/classification.js`, since a 10-second short-foot hold and a 45-second ball roll are not interchangeable.
- **Optional warm-up, off by default.** When turned on, it runs ankle circles and the knee-to-wall drill up front and leaves them out of the random draw so they never appear twice in one session; with the toggle off, they can still be drawn into the main list like any other mobility-group exercise.
- **Time budget.** `maxCount` bounds the exercise count using the single most time-consuming exercise in the pool as a safe (worst-case) per-exercise cost, the same conservative approach Hip Opener uses for one-sided poses, so a session is never generated over the time available.
- Every exercise is an `instruction` variant with a shared neutral foot icon (`media/examples/foot.svg`) until illustrations are ready.
