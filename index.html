Climbing

Hangboard and pull-up bar training, labelled by what each protocol trains. Pick a goal, then start a protocol. You set the load (added weight, a smaller edge, or feet on a chair to assist); the app handles the timing.

How to fit it into a week
  • Fingers first, while fresh. Always do the warm-up hangs; never start cold on max hangs.
  • Two hard finger sessions a week, at least 48 hours apart. That is what the hangboard studies used, for 4–8 weeks. Low-intensity hangs are light enough for most days.
  • One focus per block. For example, 4 weeks of max hangs, then 4 weeks of repeaters or intermittent hangs. Retest before switching.
  • Progress slowly. Add about 1–2 kg, or move to a slightly smaller edge, only when every hang feels solid.
  • Balance the pulling with an antagonist session each week.
  • Stop for sharp or localized finger pain. Hangboarding is generally advised only after about a year of regular climbing, and not for young climbers who are still growing.

How exercises are organized

Difficulty estimates the movement’s demands. Strain estimates effort for a short, controlled set; pace, resistance, time, and rest change it. Group labels show the highest movement rating.

Arms includes shoulders and upper back. Legs includes hips. You can change any exercise’s ratings in its details.
